# The earlier Figure 4 / Figure 7 study (`fig47/`)

> This file is generated by `tools/clrs_docs_generate.py` from the repository and the training outputs; do not edit it by hand. Re-run the generator after any change to the data.


Before the edge-site transcoders, the project reproduced the two central figures of the Anthropic circuit-tracing paper for all twelve algorithms using the node-site transcoder (192 features, 8 active). This document explains what each result file in `fig47/` contains and tabulates the numbers.

## The two figures

- **Figure 4 (one example):** pick one real graph and one real prediction; group the features into a few named supernodes; show the supernodes feeding the prediction; then clamp one supernode (to -2 times its value) and show what the real network does.
- **Figure 7 (validation sweep):** repeat the clamp over many examples and many supernodes to see whether the story of one example survives. Here: 15 independently sampled graphs per algorithm.

## The recurring-feature question

Verdict: **WEAK** (2 of 4 algorithms confirm). Confirming: bfs, topological_sort. Not confirming: dag_shortest_paths, mst_prim.

Downgraded finding: the within-category recurring-feature pattern found for bfs and topological_sort (both 100% recurrence) does NOT generalize to
dag_shortest_paths or mst_prim (50% and 67% recurrence, below the 80% threshold), the two other CLEAN-class algorithms tested for this specific mechanism. bfs
and topological_sort should be treated as isolated cases showing a specific recurring-feature pattern, not evidence of a general within-category-redundancy
mechanism across CLEAN algorithms.
